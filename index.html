How it works

Each copy is an OpLog from egwalker-swift. It records every edit as a plain position, such as “insert at 5”, together with the edits its author had already seen. Nothing else is stored.

Reconnecting calls merge(from:), which copies over the edits a copy hasn't seen yet. The text you see is produced by checkout(), which replays the history. Where edits happened at the same time, it builds CRDT state for that stretch only and discards it afterwards. The summary above the history shows how much of the replay needed it.

In the history, newest edits are at the top. Each row is a run of consecutive edits by one person, placed in that person's column. Lines lead to the edits a run was based on, and a hollow dot marks a merge: its author had seen both branches.

Source: TobiasRe/egwalker-swift (MIT). The algorithm is from Joseph Gentle and Martin Kleppmann, Collaborative Text Editing with Eg-walker (EuroSys 2025).
